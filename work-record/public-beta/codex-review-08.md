## Standards

1. **P2 — Health reads are understated.** **Policy: 21–26.** `WorkoutTracker/Domain/HealthKitHeartRateProvider.swift:63–69` requests heart rate, active energy and basal energy; `:116–121` adds walking/running, cycling or rowing distance; `:178–205` consumes those readings. The policy names only heart rate. The generated permission string is similarly incomplete (`WorkoutTracker.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj:641,676`); matching that string does not establish accuracy. **Correction:** name heart rate, active/resting energy and supported workout-distance readings, their local display/history use, and workout writes. Qualify “never sent” as no automatic HealthKit upload: a user can deliberately include Health information in feedback text/screenshots or an export.

2. **P1 — Apple map requests are missing from the local-only location account.** **Policy: 21–28,79,120.** `WorkoutTracker/Features/Cardio/CardioCards.swift:367,382–388,472–480` derives a geographic rectangle from the recorded route, and `:529–537` asks `MKMapSnapshotter` for imagery of that rectangle. The polyline itself is drawn locally (`:407–438`); this is not evidence that the full route is uploaded. Nevertheless, “these stay on your iPhone” omits the route-area map request. **Correction:** disclose Apple Maps imagery requests for the route's area, while retaining the narrower statement that the developer receives no automatic route upload. Do not automatically change the Location label to Yes solely for Apple's own collection: [Apple's App Privacy guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/) distinguishes Apple's framework collection from data the developer collects. Document that distinction instead of using “routes stay on the device” as the complete rationale.

3. **P2 — Future qualifications do not cover the summary and all dependent statements.** **Policy: 15–17,34–45,65–67,79,137–139.** The AI heading correctly says “after ticket 06's app switch,” but the summary unconditionally says requests go through the developer's server. Currently `WorkoutTracker/Features/Gyms/TerraAccess.swift:12` reads the device key and `WorkoutTracker/Domain/TerraAPI.swift:22–27` sends directly to OpenAI. Account UI remains future work, and feedback currently gets `FeedbackDetails.forThisPhone` (`WorkoutTracker/Features/Settings/SettingsView.swift:85–87`), rather than a wired signed-in account. The committed server URL is blank (`Config/Shared.xcconfig:17–21`). **Correction:** explicitly identify the whole policy/table as the intended external-test build's behavior, or qualify the summary and signed-in feedback claims too. Record the current direct-device AI flow in the draft's implementation-status note. Do not suggest that deleting only the marked AI section makes the remaining text accurate for this branch.

4. **P2 — Uninstall is not deletion of every copy.** **Policy: 23,91.** The app writes workouts into Health (`WorkoutTracker/Domain/HealthKitHeartRateProvider.swift:135–148`) and hands exported files to a share sheet (`WorkoutTracker/Features/Settings/ExportView.swift:84–89`). These copies are outside its own workout container. The current device API key is a separate Keychain item with an explicit removal path (`WorkoutTracker/Features/Gyms/AskAIKeyStore.swift:28–42`); do not equate container deletion with guaranteed credential removal. **Correction:** say deleting the app removes its local app data, and explain that Health records, exported/shared copies, device backups and server data have separate deletion controls. Scope the promise to the shipping build after legacy-key removal is verified.

5. **P2 — The consent publishing checklist covers too few disclosures.** **Policy: 48–49,137–138.** D60/spec require all three disclosures to name both recipients. Existing consent is remembered locally under three v1 flags (`WorkoutTracker/Features/Gyms/TerraAccess.swift:4–9`). Besides the camera/photo usage strings (`WorkoutTracker.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj:640–643,675–678`), the photo screen, routine screen, model alert and Settings still name OpenAI alone (`WorkoutTracker/Features/Gyms/Scan/ScanSteps.swift:32`; `WorkoutTracker/Features/Templates/AIRoutine/AIEquipmentStep.swift:209–241`; `WorkoutTracker/Features/Gyms/ModelPicker.swift:579–583`; `WorkoutTracker/Features/Settings/AskAISettingsSheet.swift:155–162`). **Correction:** make verification of all three disclosures and their real send gates a ticket-06 publication prerequisite. Explicitly resolve how existing consent is handled when adding a new recipient. Describe separate remembered permissions, not a new prompt on every request.

6. **P2 — “Health: No” ignores intentionally supplied body measurements.** **Policy: 113–114.** `server/migrations/0005_training_profiles.sql:11–16`, `server/src/training.ts:78–90` and the routine payload in `server/src/ai.ts:246–281` store/forward optional height and body weight. Those values are distinct from the lifted weights that remain local. Apple's Health category includes user-provided health data, not just HealthKit. **Correction:** declare collected, linked Health for these body measurements in the intended profile/proxy build; retain Fitness for exercise goals, experience and schedule. Explain that HealthKit-derived workout readings are not automatically uploaded. This classification follows [Apple's category definitions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), applied to these explicit fields.

7. **P2 — Both App Privacy definitions are materially inaccurate.** **Policy: 89,105,124–127.** Apple's collection definition requires access beyond real-time request servicing; “even briefly” is incorrect. Linkage can use an account, device or other identifying details. `server/src/feedback.ts:105–123,159–170` stores arbitrary message text and image bytes without anonymizing their content; a null `account_id` is not anonymity. **Correction:** replace both definitions using [Apple's guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/). Say signed-out feedback is not automatically attached to an account but may identify its author. Do not justify “not linked” by absence of login. Keep AI inputs disclosed on the evidence available: `store:false` (`server/src/ai.ts:365–371`) does not establish zero provider retention; [OpenAI's data-controls documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data) describes default abuse-monitoring retention of up to 30 days, subject to exceptions. Replace the draft's old OpenAI link, which redirects to a general enterprise-privacy page, with that actual API retention reference.

8. **P2 — The table omits request diagnostics and uses nonexistent purpose choices.** **Policy: 113–119.** `server/migrations/0004_ai.sql:24–32` and `server/src/ai.ts:437–444` keep account-linked status, latency and token counts, even when no feedback is submitted. **Correction:** include AI latency under Performance Data and classify technical outcome diagnostics, rather than limiting diagnostics to feedback. Apple's purposes place customer support under App Functionality; “Customer Support” is a data type, and “Developer's Communications” is not a purpose. Personalized routine suggestions also warrant Product Personalization. Resolve Analytics according to actual use of the counts, not as a synonym for rate limiting. Use [Apple's data-use definitions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/). Also resolve the retained daily IP-derived anti-abuse identifier (`server/src/feedback.ts:58–73,144–146`) and any retained invocation metadata before finalizing the table; their existence is not covered merely by “feedback diagnostics.”

9. **P2 — Cloudflare log retention is wrong, and the no-content assurance is unverified.** **Policy: 75–77,133–136.** `server/wrangler.jsonc:8` enables observability without disabling invocation logs. The linked [Workers Logs documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logs/workers-logs/) currently states **3 days on Free, 7 on Paid**, and describes request/response metadata and a method/URL invocation message. The draft says Free keeps logs for seven days. The source's counts-only application logging does not prove the platform logs contain no client-supplied information. **Correction:** correct the plan-specific retention, separate application logs from platform metadata, and leave exact fields/access as an explicit deployment verification gate. Narrow “address itself is not stored” to the application's feedback tables until platform behavior is established. Do not publish “never what you sent” on the strength of a bracketed promise to inspect later.

10. **P2 — TestFlight collection is broader than optional screenshots.** **Policy: 15–17,70,79,110–112,118–119.** The target is email-invited external TestFlight testers (`work-record/public-beta/spec.md:179–187`; ticket 08 Goal/Scope). This is a distribution-service flow, not a missing Swift network call. [Apple's TestFlight privacy notice](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/test-flight/) says crash logs and usage information are automatically shared with the developer, with invitation identity and device/build details; feedback has its own retention. **Correction:** add a separate beta-distribution paragraph explaining this automatic collection, identity visibility, developer use/retention and deletion/contact route. Name/email are therefore not received only when someone signs into Stacked. Resolve the table's scope and the data the developer receives from Apple, including crash/usage diagnostics, against the release being described; do not imply the in-app feedback footer is the complete diagnostic inventory.

11. **P3 — “Export it” reads as a complete export of all preceding settings.** **Policy: 21–23.** `WorkoutTracker/Domain/ExportSnapshot.swift:47–55` makes heart-rate series JSON-only; `:111–129` enumerates exported preferences, excluding appearance and AI-consent UserDefaults. `WorkoutTracker/Domain/ExportCSV.swift:73–105` is a set/cardio ledger, not the complete JSON snapshot. **Correction:** say workout data can be exported as JSON or CSV, with JSON carrying the fuller history/route data; avoid promising every setting or identical coverage in both formats. The share destination is chosen by the user and may itself be an external service.

## Spec

12. **P2 — Account deletion overpromises revocation and synchronous screenshot removal.** **Policy: 39–40,86–88.** The spec's Accounts section expressly requires failed revocations to be “queued (encrypted, unlinked)” and a manual remedy. `server/src/index.ts:74–105` can return `pending` or `manual`; `server/src/store.ts:110–114,134–165` moves encrypted tokens into `pending_revocations`, with exponential retry delays capped at a day and cleanup after roughly 30 days. The hourly job processes only 20 due rows (`:170–175`), so this is not a strict wall-clock deletion guarantee. R2 deletion can fail and be queued too (`server/src/feedback.ts:187–212,220–249`). **Correction:** distinguish immediate live-database account deletion from asynchronous screenshot removal and retained, account-unlinked revocation credentials. Explain possible permanent revocation failure and the iOS Settings remedy. Do not promise that a successful account deletion response proves Apple access and every screenshot have already disappeared.

13. **P2 — Daily AI retention is understated; cleanup times are presented too exactly.** **Policy: 60–61,68–69.** `server/src/ai.ts:557` removes usage dates strictly before the New York date 48 hours earlier: ordinarily today plus the preceding two calendar dates remain, approximately two to three days, with DST boundary effects. `:558` makes request logs older than 90 days eligible for pruning. `server/src/feedback.ts:249` removes feedback counters from earlier New York dates. All are scheduled at minute 23 each hour (`server/wrangler.jsonc:11`; `server/src/index.ts:189–192`) and can fail. **Correction:** describe the actual usage-date cutoff and removal on the next successful hourly cleanup; describe 90 days as the request-log retention target, and signed-out codes as eligible after the next New York midnight. “Daily counts are kept for two days” is not the implemented rule.

14. **P2 — Session expiry is not session-record deletion.** **Policy: 37–43.** The 90-day inactivity expiry is substantially correct, but `server/migrations/0001_accounts.sql:20–25` stores account-linked hashes plus creation, expiry and last-use timestamps. `server/src/store.ts:89–95,101–102,156` removes an expired row only when its token is presented, on signout, or on account deletion; the scheduled handler does not prune sessions. **Correction:** distinguish authorization expiry from stored-record retention, including timestamps. State that account/identity/profile records remain until deletion (the training profile can also be explicitly cleared through `server/src/training.ts:78–79`). Do not infer a 90-day database TTL from the session's validity period.

15. **P3 — Exercise-suggestion payload omits typed manufacturer/model.** **Policy: 51–54.** `server/src/ai.ts:284–300` forwards the entered brand and model even without OCR, alongside label lines and exercise IDs/names/muscle groups. The current app's disclosure already says so (`WorkoutTracker/Features/Gyms/ModelPicker.swift:583`); D53 expressly identifies manufacturer/model as typed. **Correction:** include manufacturer/model entered by the user, not just text read from a label. The scan and routine payload descriptions otherwise correspond to their structured inputs; no automatic workout-history/HealthKit payload was found.

16. **P2 — Server deletion/retention omit recovery copies and downloaded feedback.** **Policy: 60–61,69,74–77,86–90,129–140.** `server/wrangler.jsonc:22–28` selects D1. [D1 Time Travel](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/reference/time-travel/) is automatic and retains recoverable history for seven days on Free or 30 on Paid; a live-table DELETE is not erasure of that history. Moreover, `server/scripts/feedback.mjs:76–84` can save screenshots locally, and `server/src/store.ts:134–165` cannot delete those copies. Ticket 08 also requires a database export procedure before external testers. **Correction:** add recovery/backup retention and developer-downloaded feedback copies to the publication checklist and deletion procedure. State the live-store scope of automated deletion, choose retention for any manual exports/downloads, and ensure a restore does not silently reinstate deleted accounts. This is an omitted lifecycle, not evidence that any production backup or screenshot download already exists.

17. **P2 — Named publishing decisions remain unresolved.** **Policy: 13,67,69,95,99,115–119,129–140.** The placeholders are appropriate for a draft, but cannot survive publication. `server/src/feedback.ts:159–175,220–254` retains valid feedback indefinitely until explicitly deleted; its sweep removes orphan/deleted screenshots and counters, not old live feedback. `server/src/pages.ts:11–16` still serves placeholder privacy/support pages. **Correction:** record the user's legal name, contact, date, age statement and approved feedback retention; implement any promised automatic retention before saying it exists. Finalize the purpose/linkage choices, production logging fields/plan, processor settings and backup/download handling. Qualify “Only I read it” as the developer's own access practice rather than an unverified guarantee about infrastructure operators. Recheck 03/04/05/06 app completion, all recipient disclosures, deployed retention/cleanup and the exact external-test build; then obtain the user's wording approval and publish both pages. These are release gates, not a request to publish or deploy during this review.

The underlying account fields, encrypted Apple-token purpose, training values/units, signed-out daily keyed-IP counter, feedback payload/re-encoding, counts-only AI application records, and live-account deletion transaction are supported by source. All five migrations were examined, including `screenshot_deletions`, `ai_account_pauses` and `pending_revocations`. No advertising, sale, cross-company tracking integration or automatic workout-history synchronization was found. “No tracking” is consistent with the audited app/server paths; it does not settle deployment-specific logging or future Google wiring. Camera/OCR processing and image re-encoding are local before the consented AI send; exports and user-submitted feedback are deliberate exceptions to broad local-data assurances.

Standards: 11 findings, highest P1 (#2). Spec: 6 findings, highest P2. The draft is not ready for independent clearance; factual corrections are needed in addition to the acknowledged user placeholders.

Verdict: not clear
