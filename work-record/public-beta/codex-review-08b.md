## Standards

1. **P3 — The new label-reading statement needs its mode qualification.** **Policy: 38–40.** “Reading the label happens on your iPhone” describes the offline reader, but AI identification also reads label text remotely. `WorkoutTracker/Domain/EquipmentIdentification.swift:19–28` instructs the model to read identifying text, and `WorkoutTracker/Features/Gyms/IdentifyEquipmentSheet.swift:312–315` sends the photo for that operation. The intended server implementation likewise sends the scan image upstream (`server/src/ai.ts:237–250,365–371`). The following AI exception reduces the ambiguity, but does not make the standalone local-processing claim accurate. **Suggested correction:** “The optional on-device label reader processes the photo on your iPhone. AI identification sends the photo for processing as described below.”

2. **P3 — “Health data are not sent” conflicts with the measurements explicitly sent.** **Policy: 83–84; compare 157.** The paragraph correctly lists optional height and weight, then says Health data are not sent. The table correctly treats those measurements as Health. `WorkoutTracker/Domain/AIRoutine.swift:62–70` includes `heightCm` and `weightKg`; `server/src/ai.ts:267–281` forwards them. There is no automatic HealthKit input in that payload, which is the narrower fact the text can promise. **Suggested correction:** “The app does not automatically include your workout history or readings from Apple Health.” This also avoids implying that user-entered goals or submitted images cannot themselves contain health/history information. Keep the table's affirmative Health disclosure for body measurements; [Apple's definition](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/) is broader than HealthKit-derived data.

The earlier phone-side findings are otherwise addressed for the stated future build: the Health read inventory, Apple Maps area requests, global build scope, current direct-device AI path, export formats and uninstall exceptions are now explained. All three AI consent disclosures, Settings, permission strings and legacy-key removal are identified as release gates. The revised collection/linkage definitions, diagnostic categories and purpose choices correct the earlier errors. Outstanding choices about usage analytics, signed-out linkage, IP-derived codes, invocation logs and TestFlight label treatment are explicitly identified; their unresolved status alone is not a finding.

## Spec

3. **P2 — A retained Apple credential is separated from the live account, not demonstrably unlinked from the person.** **Policy: 122–126.** “No longer linked to you” overstates the deletion guarantee. `server/src/store.ts:140–142` copies the **same ciphertext** from `identities.refresh_token_enc` into `pending_revocations`. The queue has no account ID, but the recovery history expressly acknowledged at policy 125–126 can still hold the original identity/account association and matching ciphertext. The spec's “queued (encrypted, unlinked)” describes separation from the live account rows, not anonymization across retained recovery copies. Also, the pending path covers decryption failures and unsuccessful Apple responses, not just an unreachable Apple endpoint (`server/src/index.ts:79–84`). **Suggested correction:** “If revocation fails, I keep the encrypted token separately from your deleted account record, solely to retry revocation.” Retain the retry period and manual iOS Settings remedy. This is a qualification of round-one finding 12, not a request to change the deletion implementation.

4. **P2 — The recovery-copy gate still omits manual database-export retention and signed-in downloaded-copy deletion.** **Policy: 102–103,110–111,120–128,178–190.** The rewrite now gives D1 Time Travel its own window and asks for screenshot/TestFlight-export retention, but the planned **manual D1 backup/export files** have no corresponding retention decision. Gate 3 asks only for an export/restore procedure that reapplies deletions. Those files are distinct from Time Travel and are not expired by its window. Ticket 08 explicitly requires a D1 export procedure (`work-record/public-beta/issues/08-external-testers.md:25–26`). Separately, the signed-out deletion promise includes downloaded screenshots at line 128, while account deletion describes live database/R2 cleanup only. `server/scripts/feedback.mjs:76–84` can download either sender's screenshot, and `server/src/store.ts:134–158` cannot remove those local files. **Suggested correction:** extend the existing release gate to decide retention/access/deletion for manual database exports and specify how downloaded **signed-in** feedback copies are removed when that account is deleted. Reflect the chosen limits in the deletion wording. A placeholder for these decisions is sufficient at this stage; no policy value or implementation is demanded in this review. No production backup or download is assumed to exist. This is the remaining scope gap in round-one finding 16. [Cloudflare's Time Travel documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/reference/time-travel/) describes the managed recovery history, not a lifecycle for separately exported files.

The other server corrections match the unchanged implementation: session validity versus stored-record removal (`server/src/store.ts:80–102`), roughly two-to-three-day usage buckets and hourly AI pruning (`server/src/ai.ts:552–563`), typed manufacturer/model inputs (`server/src/ai.ts:284–300`), and queued screenshot cleanup (`server/src/feedback.ts:187–254`). The retry/manual-remedy disclosure is adequate without requiring exact cron-boundary promises. The account, identity, training-profile, feedback, AI-usage/request, revocation and screenshot-deletion storage paths retain the round-one source findings; no newly added data flow or schema was found.

The draft appropriately leaves legal name/contact/date, age statement, feedback/download retention, exact production logging fields and plan, consent migration, deployed cleanup checks, approved pages and final release-build review to the publishing process. [Apple's TestFlight notice](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/test-flight/) supports the added beta-distribution paragraph; [OpenAI's data-controls documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data) supports retaining the provider-retention qualification despite `store:false`. These checks do not establish deployment settings or user approval.

Standards: 2 findings, highest P3. Spec: 2 findings, highest P2. Most round-one issues are resolved; the remaining findings concern wording and one incomplete lifecycle gate, not the expected placeholders themselves.
